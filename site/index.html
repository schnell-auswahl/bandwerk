Split your bass in two.

Hothouse Bass Multiband turns the Cleveland Music Co. Hothouse into a multiband bass processor. A crossover splits your signal in two: keep the lows clean, tight and solid, and compress, loop, octave and drive the highs. Open source, free, installed from your browser.

Effect 01 · K3 · every page

Crossover.

The crossover decides where your bass is split. Everything below the crossover point goes to the low band, everything above it to the high band. Low settings keep only the deepest fundamentals clean; higher settings put more of the body of your tone into the clean low band and leave only the bite for the effects.

Hz at 12 o’clock
250
Hz range
100–800
dB per octave, each band
24
Sum of both bands
Flat

How it works

A Linkwitz-Riley crossover of the 4th order: each band is two Butterworth filters in series, 24 dB per octave. At the crossover point both bands are 6 dB down and exactly in phase, so when they are added back together the level is the same at every frequency. With all effects neutral the pedal sounds like your bass.

K3 is two-part logarithmic: fully left is 100 Hz, 12 o’clock exactly 250 Hz, fully right 800 Hz. The frequency glides smoothly while you turn, without zipper noise. Anything that delays one band (the drive’s oversampling, the FX loop) delays the other band by exactly the same amount, so the sum stays flat.

K3 is live on every page. At power-up it starts at its saved value: turn it until LED 1 stops blinking, then it follows the knob.

Starting points

  • About 170 Hz (K3 at 10 o’clock). Only the fundamentals of the lower strings stay clean. Tight, modern drive tones.
  • 250 Hz (12 o’clock). The all-rounder: the low band carries the body, the high band the growl.
  • 500–800 Hz. Almost all of your tone stays clean; drive and octave up only add an edge on top. Subtle grit for fingerstyle.

Effect 02 · Page COMP · move S1

Compressors.

Two compressors, one per band, always in the signal path. The low compressor holds your fundamental at a steady level, so every note, open E or 12th fret, sits in the same place in the mix. The high compressor evens out pick and slap attack and feeds the drive a steady signal, so the distortion doesn’t collapse when you play softly.

Max ratio, low band
20:1
Max ratio, high band
8:1
ms attack, low / high
10/5
dB lowest threshold
−40

How it works

A feed-forward design. A peak detector measures the level in dB; it holds each peak briefly (25 ms low, 5 ms high), so the long waveforms of low notes don’t make the gain wobble within every cycle. Above the threshold the level is reduced by the ratio, with a 6 dB soft knee. Attack is fixed (10 ms low, 5 ms high), release is on K5.

Auto makeup gain brings the compressed signal back up: threshold × (1 − 1/ratio) × 0.6, so it gives back 60 % of what a full-scale note loses. Dry/wet then blends the compressed signal with the untouched one, parallel compression that keeps your transients.

Moving S1 opens the COMP page and chooses what the knobs edit: up = HIGH band, middle = both, down = LOW band. With both selected, one knob moves both bands to the same knob position, each within its own range.

Starting points

  • The defaults. The low band is held firmly (8:1), the high band gently and in parallel (3:1, 60 % wet). A solid start for almost everything.
  • Even fingerstyle. S1 down, threshold (K6) around 10 o’clock (about −30 dB): low notes stop jumping out.
  • More click and attack. S1 up, dry/wet (K4) at about 10 o’clock: the compressed signal fills in under untouched transients.
  • Compression off. Turn a band’s dry/wet (K4) fully left.

Effect 03 · Page LOOP · move S2

FX loop.

Put any effect pedal on the high band only: a chorus, a phaser, an envelope filter, your favourite distortion. The low band bypasses the loop and stays clean and solid. The pedal measures how long the round trip through your effects takes and delays the low band by the same time, so both bands meet again in phase.

Latency measurement

dB send and return level

±1
ms longest loop latency
≈20

How it works

The high band leaves the pedal on the send right after its compressor. What comes back on the return is blended with a dry copy of the high band (K4: fully left = dry only, fully right = return only, a plain serial loop). The octave up and the drive come after the loop.

Why phase alignment matters. Digital pedals delay the signal, often by one to a few milliseconds. If the high band comes back late and the low band doesn’t, the two bands no longer add up properly around the crossover point, and a dry/wet blend turns into a comb filter that sounds hollow and thin (see the graph). So the low band and the dry part of the blend are delayed by exactly the loop latency, to a fraction of a sample.

Automatic measurement. At power-up (one second after start, if S2 isn’t on Bridge) and every time you move S2 from Bridge to a loop mode, the pedal sends a short burst of noise (−20 dBFS, about 43 ms) to the send and finds it in the return by cross-correlation. This takes about a tenth of a second: meanwhile the high band plays dry, your output never goes silent, and the noise is not mixed into your output. The result is saved. If nothing comes back (effect off or still booting, a noise gate), the last good value stays, LED 1 blinks fast for 1.5 s, and the pedal tries again every 2 s, up to 15 times.

Starting points

  • Serial (K4 fully right) for most pedals. Blend (K4 around 12 o’clock) for drives and fuzzes when you want to keep some clean high band.
  • Changed a preset or swapped pedals? Flip S2 to Bridge and back: the pedal measures again.
  • Check the alignment by ear. Neutral effect, S2 down, K4 at 12 o’clock: turn K6 until the sound is thinnest, then S2 back to the middle.
  • Limits. A delay or reverb in the loop repeats the measurement noise audibly; a noise gate may swallow it.

Effect 04 · Page OCTAVE · FS1

Octaver.

One octave down for synth-like weight, one octave up for a bright, ringing edge. It is built like the classic analog octavers, so it follows your playing instantly and has their character, quirks included. The sub octave joins the low band; the octave up joins the high band right before the drive, so switching both on gives you a classic octave fuzz.

Octave, flip-flop sub
−1
Octave, rectifier
+1
% max level, each
200
Hz sub tone range
80–500

How it works

A detector listens to your full signal before the crossover (you never hear it). It evens out the level, filters away everything above about 300 Hz so only the fundamental remains, and watches for zero crossings, with hysteresis so noise can’t trigger it twice.

−1 octave. A flip-flop changes state at every rising zero crossing, which makes a square wave at exactly half your note’s frequency. The sub tone filter (K6) rounds it off from buzzy to round, and it follows the volume of your playing.

+1 octave. Full-wave rectifying the signal (folding its negative half up) doubles its frequency. The signal is band-limited before (800 Hz) and shaped after (90 Hz to 3 kHz), then levelled to match.

A gate switches both octaves off below about −50 dBFS, so the sub doesn’t chatter on noise between notes. FS1 switches the octaver on and off with a short ramp, no clicks.

Tracking limits. Monophonic, like every analog octaver: play single notes. Chords and double stops confuse the −1 (the +1 copes better, it is a pure rectifier). The sub needs a clear fundamental: very soft or dead notes and harmonic-heavy playing (slap, right at the bridge) can make it warble or drop out.

Dual taper: at 12 o’clock your direct signal and the octaves both play at full level. Fully left = direct only, fully right = octaves only.

Starting points

  • Synth bass. −1 at 12 o’clock, +1 fully left, sub tone at 9 o’clock, dry/wet a little right of 12.
  • Octave fuzz. +1 at 2 o’clock, −1 fully left, drive on in FUZZ: the octave up goes straight into the fuzz.
  • Steadiest sub. Play cleanly with your fingers, nearer the neck than the bridge.

Effect 05 · Page DRIVE · FS2 · S3

Drive.

Three flavours of dirt, overdrive, distortion and fuzz, on the high band only. Your low end stays clean and solid underneath, so you can go much further than with a full-range drive without losing the bottom. A level match keeps the volume about the same when you switch it on.

Beta The voicing (gain ranges, loudness match) is provisional in v0.1 and will be tuned by ear.

Oversampling, dist and fuzz
4×

How it works

Gain (K2) pushes the high band into a clipping curve; S3 picks the curve. Overdrive (up) is a soft tanh curve with a slight asymmetry for warm even harmonics. Distortion (middle) stays clean longer, then flattens firmly. Fuzz (down) clips hard and off-centre for a spitty, ragged edge.

Oversampling, in plain words. Clipping creates new overtones, some of them higher than 48 kHz audio can hold. Without precautions they fold back down as inharmonic, metallic noise (aliasing). So the drive works at 2× (overdrive) or 4× (distortion, fuzz) the sample rate internally and filters before it comes back down. It always delays by the same 18 samples (0.375 ms), on or off, and the low band is delayed to match.

Tone (K6) is a tilt EQ around 800 Hz after the clipping. Level (K5) sets the output: 12 o’clock is about as loud as the drive switched off.

Fuzz split. In fuzz mode, with the drive on, a second crossover at half the crossover frequency takes the upper part of the low band (with K3 at 250 Hz: 125 to 250 Hz) and feeds it into the fuzz too. The fuzz gets more body; the deepest lows stay clean.

Starting points

  • Modern growl. DIST, gain at 1 o’clock, tone a bit right, K3 at 10 o’clock (about 170 Hz).
  • Vintage fuzz. FUZZ, gain at 12, tone a bit left, K3 at 12: the fuzz split adds body from the upper lows.
  • Just some hair. OD, gain at 9 o’clock, band mix (K4) at 12.

Effect 06 · K1 every page · K4 on page DRIVE

Mix & master.

At the end both bands come back together. The band mix leans the balance towards the clean lows or the processed highs, the master sets the output level, and a soft clipper makes sure the pedal never clips harshly, however hard you push it.

dB, both bands at 12 o’clock
0
dB max master
+6
dBFS where soft clip starts
−3
ms bypass crossfade
10

How it works

Band mix (K4 on page DRIVE, active whether the drive is on or off): at 12 o’clock both bands play at 0 dB. Turning left fades the high band out while the low band stays at 0 dB; turning right fades the low band out. Halfway to either end the fading band is about 12 dB down.

Master (K1, every page): from silence up to +6 dB, 0 dB at 12 o’clock. It is always live and follows the knob from the moment the pedal starts.

Soft clip. Up to about −3 dBFS the output is untouched; above that a smooth curve rounds off the peaks instead of hard digital clipping.

Bypass. Tap both footswitches together. Your bass goes straight to the output, untouched by any processing, with a 10 ms crossfade. Everything keeps running in the background, so switching back on doesn’t pump or click. The FX send is muted while bypassed, so pedals in the loop get no signal. Both LEDs are off in bypass.

Starting points

  • Hear each band alone. K4 fully left = low band only, fully right = high band only.
  • Match your bypass level. Set the master so the pedal is as loud on as off; the soft clipper is a safety net, not a sound.
  • Recording direct. Keep the master at or below 12 o’clock and your peaks under the soft-clip point.

Pedal guide.

Six knobs, three toggle switches, two footswitches, two LEDs, no screen. Here is how they reach every parameter.

Pages: four sets of knobs

K1 (master) and K3 (crossover) do the same thing on every page and are always live. K2, K4, K5 and K6 change their job with the page: COMP, LOOP, OCTAVE or DRIVE. The pedal starts on the DRIVE page.

Knob pickup

When you change page, K2, K4, K5 and K6 keep their stored values; nothing jumps to wherever the knob happens to point. Turn a knob and LED 1 blinks: slowly while the knob is far from the stored value, faster (up to about 10 times a second) as you get closer. As soon as you reach or pass the stored value the knob takes over, and LED 1 stays lit while you turn it.

K3 works the same way once after power-up, because the crossover starts at its saved value. On the COMP page with both bands selected, the pickup point is the average of the two bands.

What is saved, and when

Every page parameter, the crossover, octaver and drive on/off, and the measured loop latency are saved to the pedal’s flash memory automatically, 3 seconds after your last change (and only if something changed). The writes are spread over the memory, good for millions of saves.

Not saved: the master (it always follows K1), bypass (the pedal always starts active), the page (always DRIVE at power-up) and the toggle switches (they are physical). There is no reset gesture on the pedal, so it can’t happen by accident.

Power-up

  1. 0 sBootloader window. For 2 seconds the pedal waits for an installer; this is what makes a failed update always recoverable. No sound yet.
  2. ≈ 2 sThe effect starts. Both LEDs blink 3 times, the saved settings are loaded, the DRIVE page is open and the master follows K1.
  3. + 1 sLoop check. If S2 is on a loop mode, the pedal measures the loop latency (about 0.1 s, your sound keeps playing).
  4. Any timePick up K3. If you want a different crossover, turn K3 until LED 1 stops blinking.

Cables & wiring.

IN and OUT are stereo (TRS) jacks used as two mono channels: the tips carry your bass and the pedal output, the rings carry the FX return and send.

JackTipRing
INBass inputFX return (from your effect’s output)
OUTPedal output (to the amp)FX send (to your effect’s input)

Insert cable labels differ between brands (“send”, “return”, colored rings): go by tip and ring, not by the label.

Using plain TS cables?

Keep S2 on Bridge (up). A TS plug grounds the ring, so in a loop mode the return is silent: the high band disappears (at full loop mix) and the latency measurement fails, LED 1 blinks fast.

Install in your browser.

No toolchain, no command line. The installer writes the firmware over USB right from this page. The first install also adds the Daisy bootloader; later updates only replace the firmware and your settings stay.

  1. 1

    Connect USB

    Connect the USB port of the Daisy Seed in your pedal to your computer with a USB data cable (some cables only charge).

  2. 2

    Update mode

    Already on Bass Multiband: the installer does it for you over USB. First install: hold both footswitches for 2 seconds in your current Hothouse firmware, or press BOOT + RESET on the Daisy Seed.

  3. 3

    Click Install

    Choose the pedal when the browser asks. On a first install it asks twice, because the pedal restarts in between. Then wait until the installer says it’s done.

Windows

If the browser can’t open the pedal in update mode, Windows has no suitable driver for it yet. Install the WinUSB driver once with Zadig: put the pedal in update mode, choose it in the list (a DFU device with the USB ID 0483:DF11), select WinUSB and click Install.

Linux

Your user needs access to the USB device 0483:DF11, usually through a udev rule.

Fallback

If the installer doesn’t work for you, download the bootloader and firmware files from the GitHub releases and flash them with the Electro-Smith Daisy Web Programmer, or with dfu-util from the command line (see Developers).

Questions.

Do I need a computer on stage?

No. Once installed, the pedal works on its own with its knobs, switches and footswitches, and it remembers your settings. The computer is only for installing, updating and, if you like, the studio.

Which browsers work?

The installer needs WebUSB: Chrome, Edge or Opera on a desktop computer, or Chrome on Android. Connecting the studio to your pedal needs Web Serial: the same browsers, plus Firefox 151 or newer on desktop. Safari and every browser on iPhone and iPad can’t talk to USB devices. The studio’s demo works everywhere.

Will an update keep my settings?

Yes. Your settings live in their own area of the pedal’s flash chip, separate from the firmware, and an update only rewrites the firmware. Should a future version ever need a new settings format, its release notes will say so, and the pedal starts from the factory settings once.

Why does the pedal wait 2 seconds before it plays?

For the first 2 seconds after power-up the Daisy bootloader waits for an installer. That window means a broken or interrupted update can always be fixed by simply power-cycling the pedal and installing again, without opening the enclosure. After it, the effect starts and both LEDs blink 3 times.

Can I brick my pedal?

Very unlikely. The 2-second bootloader window is there at every power-up. And if everything else fails, BOOT + RESET on the Daisy Seed always reaches the chip’s built-in bootloader, which can’t be overwritten.

Why is the FX loop on the high band only?

So your low end never goes through effects that could thin it out, add noise or add latency. Only the high band goes out; the pedal delays the low band to match whatever comes back.

How do I go back to another Hothouse firmware?

Most Hothouse firmwares are installed through the STM32 system bootloader into the Daisy’s internal flash. Hold BOOT and press RESET on the Daisy Seed inside the pedal (in Bass Multiband, holding both footswitches opens the Daisy bootloader instead), then install the other firmware the way its author describes, for example with the Daisy Web Programmer. This replaces the Daisy bootloader too; to come back later, use the installer here like a first install.

What does “beta” mean here?

Version 0.1 is complete and tested on the bench, but two parts are still open: the FX loop has not been verified on hardware with real pedals yet, and the drive voicing (gain ranges, loudness match) is provisional. Reports are very welcome on GitHub.
